## Post 2: Weekly digest that actually reads your notes

The digest "Reflexion" section went through 8+ iterations to stop being abstract and start referencing real note content.

### Short
> Shipping a weekly AI digest is easy. Shipping one that isn't generic garbage took me 8 rewrites.
>
> Echo's digest now pulls from your actual entries. Concrete topics only. No "you seem to be on a journey."

### Medium
> The hardest feature in Echo wasn't the chat. It was the weekly digest.
>
> First version: vague motivational summary. Useless.
> Version 3: psychologist lens. Pretentious.
> Version 5: too casual, felt like a stranger guessing.
> Version 8: references specific things you wrote, covers the whole week, no assumptions about your fears or motivations.
>
> Sometimes the feature is easy to build and hard to make good.

### Long
> Building in public: the evolution of Echo's weekly digest.
>
> The idea is simple. You journal all week, and on Sunday Echo sends you a reflection. It reads everything you wrote and gives you a useful summary.
>
> In practice, every version was bad in a different way.
>
> V1 gave generic affirmations. "You're clearly someone who values growth." I don't need AI for that.
> V3 tried a psychologist/sociologist interpretive lens. Way too heavy for a journal app.
> V5 went full casual. "Hey bestie, wild week huh?" No.
> V8 finally works. It pulls concrete topics from your entries, covers the full week, and never assumes what you're feeling or why.
>
> The rule that fixed it: "reference what's written, not what you think it means."
>
> Same rule that made the chat good. Turns out the whole product is just one principle applied consistently.

## Post 3: Indie dev + landing page + privacy angle

Echo's landing page and the overall product philosophy: private, no ads, no tracking, AI that knows you.

### Short
> Building an AI journal app with one rule: your thoughts are not a product.
>
> No ads. No tracking. No "share with friends." Just you and an AI that actually remembers what you wrote.
>
> Echo. Coming to Android.

### Medium
> Most note apps store what you write. Echo understands it.
>
> You write about your day, a recipe, an argument, a decision you keep putting off. Echo reads all of it, builds context over time, and answers questions from your own words.
>
> Voice or text. English or Spanish. Private by default.
>
> No ads, no tracking, no social features. An indie app built for one person at a time.
>
> Landing page is live. Android app coming soon.

### Long
> 6 months into building Echo and the product finally feels right.
>
> The pitch: an AI journaling companion that actually reads your notes. Not a chatbot with a journal skin. Not a productivity app with AI sprinkled on top. A companion that knows what you wrote last Tuesday and brings it up when it matters.
>
> What I got right early: voice + text input, bilingual EN/ES, weekly digest.
> What took forever: making the AI useful instead of just impressive.
>
> The landing page went through its own journey. Started functional, ended up with a dark aesthetic, dither shaders, scroll animations. "Think out loud. Echo remembers." Still my favorite line.
>
> Tech stack for the curious: React + Vite frontend, Node/Express backend, Supabase for data, Claude Haiku for AI, Capacitor for Android. Deployed on Railway. Total infra cost: less than a Netflix subscription.
>
> No investors. No team. No ads. Just an indie app that treats your journal like it matters.
>
> If you journal (or want to start), Echo is for you.
