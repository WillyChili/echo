### Medium

A week of invisible improvements on Echo.

Redesigned the navigation bar. Moved the language selector to where people actually look for it. Made settings save silently in the background instead of showing a confirmation toast every time. Fixed note sorting so today's entries always show first. Chat now jumps to the bottom instantly instead of a slow scroll animation.

None of these are features you'd put on a landing page. But they're the difference between an app that feels like a prototype and one that feels like a product.

Polish is underrated. #buildinpublic #indiedev #mobiledev

### Long

The unglamorous side of shipping an app.

I spent this week on Echo doing work that no user will ever consciously notice, and that's exactly the point.

The navigation bar was off-center by a few pixels. Fixed it with a proper grid layout. The language toggle was buried inside "Edit Profile" where nobody could find it. Moved it to the main settings screen. Every time you changed a setting, a toast popped up saying "Saved!" Like, yeah, I know. Now it saves silently.

Notes were sorting wrong when you had multiple entries on the same day. Chat was doing a smooth scroll to the bottom on every load, which felt sluggish. Removed the "New Note" button because the app auto-creates today's note anyway.

Each fix took 10-30 minutes. Together they transformed how the app feels. This is the work that separates apps people try once from apps people keep.

Building Echo, an AI journaling companion for Android. More polish ahead.

### Long

How do you convince a stranger to trust an AI with their journal?

That's the question I wrestled with while rebuilding Echo's landing page this week.

Echo is an AI journaling app. You write notes, Echo remembers context, and you can chat with an AI that actually knows what's going on in your life. The product is solid. The landing page wasn't.

The old page listed features: "Voice input. Weekly digests. AI chat." Technically accurate. Emotionally flat. It read like a spec sheet for something nobody asked for.

The new page starts with a problem everyone recognizes: you think of something important during the day, and by evening it's gone. Then it shows how Echo solves that. Not with bullet points, but with a narrative that mirrors the actual experience of using the app.

I added a Trust section because if someone's going to write their real thoughts in an app, they need to believe it's safe. And I replaced every "Coming soon" button with functional CTAs because placeholder buttons are just broken promises in disguise.

The result: a page that explains what Echo does in 5 seconds and makes you want to try it in 10.

Still iterating. Always iterating. #buildinpublic #indiedev #landing #androiddev
